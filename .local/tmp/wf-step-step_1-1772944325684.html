From the prairie to the present

Today, that same spirit drives Iowa forward — through innovation in agriculture, a growing tech sector, and communities that still look out for one another the way they always have.

From Prairie to Statehood

Iowa entered the Union on December 28, 1846, becoming the 29th state. Before statehood, the land was home to the Meskwaki, Sioux, and other Indigenous nations who understood its rhythms long before European settlers arrived.

The settlers who came in the mid-1800s found a landscape of extraordinary promise — deep black soil, gentle rivers, and skies that seemed to go on forever. They built towns, planted fields, and forged a culture defined by hard work, honesty, and an abiding sense of community.

That culture endures. Iowa today is a state that honors its roots while embracing the future — a place where family farms stand alongside wind turbines, and where the Iowa Writers' Workshop has shaped American literature for nearly a century.

With a literacy rate that consistently ranks among the nation's highest and a quality of life that draws people from across the country, Iowa is proof that the heartland is not a relic — it is a living, breathing promise.

History

Iowa through the ages

Pre-1800s

Indigenous Nations

The Meskwaki, Sioux, and Potawatomi peoples inhabit the land, living in harmony with the prairie and its rivers for thousands of years.

1838

Iowa Territory Established

Congress creates the Iowa Territory, drawing settlers westward with the promise of fertile land and new beginnings.

1846

Statehood

Iowa becomes the 29th state on December 28, 1846. Iowa City serves as the first state capital before Des Moines takes the role in 1857.

1936

Iowa Writers' Workshop Founded

The University of Iowa establishes the world's first and most celebrated creative writing MFA program, launching a literary legacy that continues today.

1980s–Present

Renewable Energy Pioneer

Iowa becomes a national leader in wind energy, generating over 60% of its electricity from wind — among the highest percentages of any state in the nation.

Feeding the World,
One Harvest at a Time

Iowa's 30 million acres of farmland produce more food and renewable energy than almost any other state in America.

Agriculture

The backbone of Iowa

Four pillars define Iowa's agricultural identity — each a story of innovation, tradition, and stewardship.

🌽

Corn

Iowa is the nation's top corn-producing state, harvesting over 2 billion bushels annually. The crop underpins everything from food production to ethanol fuel, making Iowa central to America's energy and food security.

#1 in U.S. Corn Production
🫘

Soybeans

Second only to corn in acreage, Iowa's soybean harvest feeds livestock across the globe and supplies the growing demand for plant-based proteins. Iowa farmers consistently rank among the nation's most productive soybean growers.

#2 in U.S. Soybean Production
🐖

Livestock

Iowa raises more hogs than any other state — nearly one-third of all pigs in the U.S. call Iowa home. The state is also a major producer of cattle, eggs, and dairy, supporting a robust and diverse livestock economy.

#1 in U.S. Hog Production
💨

Renewable Energy

Iowa's flat terrain and persistent winds make it a natural powerhouse for wind energy. With over 5,000 wind turbines, Iowa generates more than 60% of its electricity from wind — a model for sustainable energy nationwide.

#2 in U.S. Wind Energy Capacity

Iowa's Wild Places

Ancient, unexpected, and breathtaking — Iowa's natural landscapes reward those who look beyond the fields.

01 — National Monument

Effigy Mounds

Rising above the Mississippi River in northeastern Iowa, Effigy Mounds National Monument preserves over 200 prehistoric mounds built by Indigenous peoples more than 2,500 years ago. Many are shaped like bears and birds — silent, sacred forms that speak across millennia.

Trails wind through ancient forests and along dramatic bluffs, offering views of the Mississippi that have remained unchanged for centuries. It is one of the most spiritually resonant landscapes in the American Midwest.

National Monument Hiking Indigenous History Mississippi River

02 — State Scenic Byway

Loess Hills

Stretching 200 miles along Iowa's western border, the Loess Hills are a geological wonder found in only two places on Earth — here and in China. Wind-deposited silt formed these steep, sculpted ridges over thousands of years, creating a landscape unlike anything else in the Midwest.

The hills harbor rare prairie ecosystems, dramatic overlooks, and a quiet that feels ancient. The Loess Hills Scenic Byway winds through small towns and sweeping vistas that reward slow travel.

Scenic Byway Prairie Ecosystems Geology Wildlife

03 — State Park

Maquoketa Caves

Iowa's most visited state park hides an extraordinary secret underground. Maquoketa Caves State Park contains the largest publicly accessible cave system in Iowa — a labyrinth of limestone passages, dramatic formations, and cathedral-like chambers carved by water over millions of years.

Above ground, forested trails lead to natural bridges and overlooks. Below, adventurers crawl through tight passages and emerge into vast caverns lit only by their own lights. It is an experience unlike any other in the Midwest.
