Our Story

A Land Shaped by
History & Heart

Iowa's story begins long before European settlement, with Indigenous peoples — the Ioway, Sauk, Meskwaki, and others — who called these fertile plains home for thousands of years. Their legacy lives on in the state's name and in the rich cultural tapestry that defines Iowa today.

Admitted to the Union on December 28, 1846, Iowa quickly became the breadbasket of a growing nation. The state's deep black soil, carved by glaciers and enriched by millennia of prairie grasses, proved ideal for agriculture that would feed millions.

Today, Iowa balances its agricultural heritage with a thriving economy, world-class universities, and a vibrant arts scene — all while maintaining the genuine warmth and community spirit that has always defined Iowans.

Key Milestones

Pre-1800s

Indigenous Peoples

The Ioway, Sauk, Meskwaki, and Sioux nations inhabit the region, establishing rich cultures along the Mississippi and Missouri rivers.

1803

Louisiana Purchase

Iowa becomes part of the United States through the Louisiana Purchase, opening the territory to American exploration and settlement.

1838

Iowa Territory

The Iowa Territory is established, with Burlington as its first capital, beginning the formal governance of the region.

1846

Statehood

Iowa becomes the 29th state of the Union on December 28, 1846, with Iowa City as the first state capital.

1857

Des Moines Capital

The state capital moves to Des Moines, which grows into a major Midwestern city and financial center.

Today

Modern Iowa

Iowa leads the nation in renewable energy, agriculture, and education, while preserving its small-town character and community values.

Places to Discover

From vibrant cities to serene natural landscapes, Iowa offers a remarkable range of experiences for every kind of traveler.

Capital City

Des Moines

A thriving capital with world-class museums, a vibrant food scene, and the iconic State Capitol building.

University Town

Iowa City

Home to the University of Iowa and a UNESCO City of Literature, brimming with culture and creativity.

National Monument

Effigy Mounds

Sacred Native American burial mounds overlooking the Mississippi River, a window into ancient history.

Natural Wonder

Loess Hills

A unique geological formation found only in Iowa and China, offering stunning vistas and rare prairie ecosystems.

Historic Village

Amana Colonies

Seven historic villages founded by German immigrants, now a National Historic Landmark with artisan shops and restaurants.

Iconic Landmark

Madison County Bridges

Six historic covered bridges immortalized in literature and film, nestled among rolling hills and autumn colors.

Farming Heritage

Feeding the World

Iowa's agricultural legacy is the foundation of its identity — a story of hard work, innovation, and stewardship of the land.

The Breadbasket of America

Iowa produces more corn and soybeans than almost any other state, contributing billions to the national economy. But Iowa agriculture is more than numbers — it's a way of life passed down through generations of farm families who understand the deep connection between land, labor, and community.

Today, Iowa farmers are also pioneers in sustainable agriculture and renewable energy, with wind turbines rising alongside corn rows across the prairie landscape.

🌽

Corn & Soybeans

Iowa ranks #1 in corn production and #2 in soybeans, growing over 2 billion bushels of corn annually on 12.5 million acres of farmland.

🌬️

Renewable Energy

Iowa generates over 60% of its electricity from wind power, leading the nation in wind energy production and sustainable farming practices.

🐷

Livestock

Iowa is the nation's top pork producer, home to more hogs than people. Cattle, dairy, and poultry operations also contribute significantly to the state's agricultural output.

🚜

Farm Innovation

Iowa State University and private companies drive agricultural innovation, from precision farming technology to drought-resistant crop varieties.

Arts & Culture

A Creative Spirit

From Grant Wood's iconic paintings to world-class festivals, Iowa's cultural life is as rich and varied as its landscape.

Iowa State Fair

One of America's oldest and largest state fairs, celebrating Iowa's culture, agriculture, and community since 1854.

🎨

Grant Wood & American Gothic

Iowa's most famous artist, Grant Wood, painted "American Gothic" in 1930, creating one of the most recognized images in American art history.

📚

Iowa Writers' Workshop

The world's most prestigious creative writing program, based at the University of Iowa, has produced dozens of Pulitzer Prize winners.

🎵

Music Heritage

Iowa is the birthplace of jazz legend Bix Beiderbecke and countless other musicians. The state's music festivals draw visitors from across the country.

🎭

Theater & Performance

From the Civic Center of Greater Des Moines to community theaters across the state, Iowa has a thriving performing arts scene.

🏛️

World-Class Museums

The Des Moines Art Center, Figge Art Museum, and State Historical Museum offer exceptional collections spanning centuries of art and history.

🎬

Film & Media

Iowa has inspired countless films and stories, from "Field of Dreams" to "The Bridges of Madison County," capturing the state's timeless beauty.
